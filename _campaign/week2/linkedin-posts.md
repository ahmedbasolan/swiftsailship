## WEDNESDAY JUNE 11 — Insight Post (No Blog Link)

### Zubair Basolan (Personal LinkedIn)

---

One customs document is killing India-UAE shipments — and most traders don't know it changed.

Since a 2025 Federal Customs Authority circular, a Certificate of Origin that hasn't been digitally transmitted through India's ICEGATE system can be treated as "unverified" at Jebel Ali. UAE customs won't necessarily reject it outright — but they'll query it. And the moment they query it, your clearance clock stops.

The situation I've seen more than once: a client has a physically valid CoO in hand. The goods arrive at Jebel Ali. Customs checks the digital record — and it's not there, because the issuing authority in India never registered for ICEGATE transmission. Now the cargo sits in a bonded warehouse while the importer scrambles to get a confirmation from the Chamber of Commerce in India.

The fix is simple but it has to happen before the shipment leaves Mumbai:

1. Confirm that the body issuing your CoO is enrolled in ICEGATE digital transmission
2. Verify that the transmitted record is visible before your vessel departs
3. Keep the ICEGATE reference number with your shipping documents

Your freight forwarder should be doing this as standard practice. If they're not — or if they don't know what ICEGATE transmission means — that's worth knowing before your next booking.

A Certificate of Origin that hasn't been digitally transmitted may not clear UAE customs — even if it's physically valid.

Since a 2025 UAE Federal Customs Authority update, Certificates of Origin on India-UAE CEPA shipments must be transmitted digitally through India's ICEGATE system. A CoO that exists only as a physical document can be flagged as "unverified" at Jebel Ali, stopping your clearance clock.

What this means in practice:

**Before your shipment leaves India:**  
→ Confirm the issuing authority (Chamber of Commerce / EIC) is enrolled in ICEGATE digital transmission  
→ Obtain the ICEGATE reference number and include it with your shipping documents  
→ Verify the digital record is visible before vessel departure — not after

This step is now standard practice on compliant India-UAE shipments. If your current freight forwarder hasn't mentioned it, ask directly.
