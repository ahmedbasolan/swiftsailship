## WEDNESDAY JUNE 4 — Insight Post (No Blog Link)

### Zubair Basolan (Personal LinkedIn)

---

The GCC-UK FTA promises 48-hour customs clearance. Here's what that actually requires from your side.

The 48-hour window starts from the moment your full documentation package is submitted — not from when the cargo arrives. Which means the clock on whether you hit that window is almost entirely in your hands, not customs'.

The documents that most commonly cause delays on the Dubai-London lane:

**Certificate of Origin** — needs to be issued by an approved body (in UAE, that's the Chamber of Commerce) and match your invoice exactly. Any discrepancy in goods description, quantity, or value triggers a query and you lose the clock.

**Commercial Invoice** — under FTA terms, this needs to include a specific "supplier's declaration" confirming the origin of the goods. Most current invoice templates don't have this field. Update your template now, before your first FTA shipment moves.

**HS Code accuracy** — the preferential rate applies at the tariff line level. If your freight forwarder is using a broad or approximate HS code, you may not be claiming the rate you're entitled to — or worse, you may be claiming one you don't qualify for.

None of this is complicated once you know it. But it's the kind of detail that sits between "the FTA says 48 hours" and "your cargo is waiting in a bonded warehouse."
